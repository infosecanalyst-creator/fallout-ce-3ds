# Welcome
[1]Fallout for New 3DS runs the Fallout Community Edition engine natively on the console. You play the original 1997 game from your own copy.
[2]Fallout 2 for New 3DS runs the Fallout 2 Community Edition engine natively on the console. You play the original 1998 game from your own copy.

The game is played on the touch screen with the stylus, the way the PC game is played with a mouse. The top screen is a dashboard: your health, action points, weapon, the enemy you are fighting and the message log. You never need to touch it.

## Reading this manual
- Tap a section on the touch screen to open it.
- Circle Pad or C-Stick: scroll. Hold to keep scrolling. You can also drag the stylus up or down on the touch screen.
- D-pad: move between the section buttons.
- L and R: previous and next section.
- LEFT at the bottom of the touch screen switches left-hand mode on or off (D-pad down to it, then A).
- B, Start or CLOSE: close. The manual opens again where you left it.

## Opening this manual
| Start | Anywhere, the main menu too: the Start menu, then MANUAL
| Select | On the main menu or any menu: open the manual
| Select+Start | On the map: open the manual (hold Select, press Start)

The game is paused while the manual is open.

# The two screens
## Bottom screen: the game
The touch screen shows the map and every menu. Use the stylus as the mouse.
- Tap to act on what you touch.
- Touch and hold for the action menu.
- Drag to move items in the inventory and when trading.

## Top screen: the dashboard
| Name, date | Your character and the game date and time. In combat the date is replaced by whose turn it is. [SNEAKING] appears after your name while sneaking
| HP bar | Green above half, amber above a quarter, red below
| AC | Armor class
| AP | Action points. Dim when exploring (your maximum); bright in combat, showing what is left this turn. "+N move" is free movement from perks or drugs
| Weapon | The item in your active hand, and its ammo. Ammo turns red when the gun is empty. The item in your other hand is shown on the right
| Attack | The current attack mode and its AP cost, for example Single shot 5 AP
| Target | In combat: the enemy you last tapped, or else the one attacking you; how hurt it is and your chance to hit it
| Level, XP | When not fighting, with your caps and carry weight on the right (red when overloaded). Poison and radiation show here instead when you have any
| Messages | The latest messages from the log, newest in bright green
| Hint | A line of button reminders along the bottom. On the map it takes turns through the shortcuts; in left-hand mode it names the buttons you really press

## The top screen on other screens
| Inventory, looting | Details of the item you last touched: damage, range, AP cost and ammo for weapons, protection for armor, weight, value and the description
| Trading | What your offer is worth, what they want, whether they will accept, their caps, and the touched item's details
| Save and load | The slot's picture, character, date, place and description
| World map | Where you are, the terrain, where you are heading, the known places, and the car's fuel
| Pip-Boy | The dashboard. Press ZL for a sharp, full-size view of the part of the Pip-Boy screen around where you last touched
| Preferences | The dashboard. Press ZL for a sharp, full-size view of the part of the page around where you last touched: the small labels under each knob are easy to read there
| Conversations | The person you are talking to and what they say
| Character sheet | The description of the stat or skill you touched
The dashboard stays on top for the Skilldex, the automap and the options menu. On any of these screens, ZL switches the top screen to a sharp, full-size view instead.

## Changing the layout
| ZL | On the map: swap screens, so the map goes on top and the original Fallout interface bar on the touch screen
| ZL | On any other screen: switch the top screen between its information and a sharp, full-size view of the part of the screen around where you last touched. Remembered for each screen
| Select+L | Whole-screen view: the entire game picture on the touch screen, the dashboard on top (ZL: a magnified lens that follows the stylus). Press again to go back
| Select+Up/Dn | Zoom the map in or out. The 3D slider zooms too
| Select+R | Sharper top screen (800 pixels wide) on or off. The choice is remembered. Not on the original 2DS

# Moving and the camera
## Walking
The Circle Pad walks your character in the direction you push, like a modern game.
- Exploring, the character keeps going while you hold the stick, turns as soon as you turn it, slides along walls, and stops when you let go.
- In combat each push takes one step to the next hex in that direction and costs 1 AP. Hold the stick to keep stepping.

You can also tap open ground to walk there. The character always runs; change that in Preferences.

## The camera
- The view follows your character and recenters near the edge of the screen.
| Select | Center the map on your character
| C-Stick | Scroll the map (New 3DS)

On an original 3DS, which has no C-Stick, the D-pad scrolls the map, so the D-pad shortcuts are not available there. Use Start, then ACTIONS: swap hands, attack mode, Skilldex and character sheet are buttons there.

# Tapping
## One tap does the right thing
A tap acts on whatever is under the stylus:
- An item on the ground: pick it up.
- A person: talk.
- A body or a container: open it to loot.
- A door, ladder, elevator or switch: use it.
- An enemy in combat: attack it. If it is out of reach, the tap walks you closer; tap again to attack. A tap only attacks someone who is fighting you, never a companion or a bystander. To attack anyone else on purpose, press B and then tap them.
- Open ground: walk there.

## The action menu
Touch and hold on anything until the menu appears. It offers what fits the object: Look, Talk, Use, Use item on and Use skill (lockpick, steal, first aid...). Slide to an icon and lift the stylus.

## Buttons that help
| R (hold) | Outline every item and lootable body on the level. In combat it also lists your chance to hit each visible enemy in the message log
| B | Use the active item: attack with your weapon, reload, or use the item in your hand on a target. Press B again to cancel and get the normal cursor back
| Select+B | Reload the weapon in your hand
| L | Right click: cycles the cursor between move, look and attack, as on the PC
| A | Left click at the cursor
| Select | On the map: center the view on your character. In a menu and on the main menu: this manual
| Start, ACTIONS | Every shortcut on this page and the next ones as a touch button: automap, rest, quick heal, reload, quick save, quick load, swap hands, attack mode, Skilldex, character sheet, end combat

# Combat
## Turns and action points
Combat is turn based. Everything you do costs action points (AP): walking 1 per hex, attacking the cost shown on the dashboard, opening the inventory 4. When your AP are spent the turn passes.

| ZR | Start combat. During combat: end your turn
| Start+ZR | End combat (only when no hostile is near)
| Tap an enemy | Attack it
| B | Attack with the active item: press B, then tap the target. B again cancels
| Select+B | Reload (costs the weapon's reload AP)
| D-pad Down | Next attack mode (single shot, burst, aimed...)
| D-pad Up | Swap hands

## Knowing your odds
The dashboard's Target line shows the last enemy you tapped: how hurt it is and your chance to hit with the current weapon and mode. Hold R to see the chance against every enemy at once.

## Healing
| Select+A | Quick heal: uses a Stimpak, or a Super Stimpak if you have no Stimpak. Costs 4 AP in combat, the same as using it from the inventory

# Inventory and trading
| X | Inventory
| X or B | Close

In the inventory, touch an item to see its details on the top screen. Drag items between your pack, the hands and the armor slot. Drop an item on your character's picture to use it. Touch and hold an item for Look, Use, Unload and Drop.

When looting a body or container, tap an item to take it (or drag it across), or press X to take everything. Tap one of your own items to put it in the container.

When trading, tap an item to put it on the table in the middle (tap it there to take it back), or drag it. The top screen shows what your offer is worth, what they want and whether they will accept. Tap Offer to make the deal. Your character's Barter skill sets the prices. For a stack you choose how many to move.

# Character and Pip-Boy
| D-pad Right | Character sheet
| D-pad Left | Skilldex
| Y | Pip-Boy
| Select+X | Automap
| Select+Y | Pip-Boy clock, to rest or wait

The button that opens a screen also closes it: Y closes the Pip-Boy, X the automap, D-pad Left the Skilldex and D-pad Right the character sheet. B closes them too.

## Character sheet
The sheet is taller than the screen. Push the Circle Pad or C-Stick down to scroll from your stats to your skills, traits and perks. Tap a stat or skill to read its description on the top screen. When you level up, tap a skill and use the plus and minus buttons to spend skill points. D-pad Right closes the sheet and keeps your changes, like tapping Done. B closes it without keeping new changes.

## Skilldex
Tap a skill (Sneak, Lockpick, Steal, Traps, First Aid, Doctor, Science, Repair), then tap the person or object to use it on.

## Pip-Boy
Your quest notes, maps, the date and the alarm clock for resting. The text is small on the touch screen: press ZL and the top screen magnifies the part around where you last touched. Pip-Boy is not available in combat.

# Talking
Tap a person to talk. Their portrait and words are on the top screen; your replies are on the touch screen as large buttons. Tap one to say it. Some replies only appear with enough Intelligence, Speech or Charisma.

Tap Barter in a conversation to trade.

# The world map
Leave a town by walking off the edge of the map. On the world map, tap where you want to go and your character travels there while the days pass. Random encounters can stop you on the way. Tap a town's circle to enter it, then pick an entrance. The top screen shows where you are, where you are heading and the places you know.

# Saving and loading
## Quick save and quick load
| Start+X | Quick save
| Start+Y | Quick load
Quick save writes at once, without a slot list and without asking for a name. It keeps to slots 7, 8 and 9: it takes an empty one first and after that overwrites the oldest quick save, so the last three quick saves are always kept. The saves are named "Quick:" and the place.

Quick save never overwrites a save you made yourself. If slots 7, 8 and 9 all hold your own saves, it says so in the message log and saves nothing: save over one of them from the Start menu, or keep your own saves in slots 1 to 6.

Quick load asks first: "Load the last quick save?" with YES and NO. The ring starts on NO and B is NO. YES loads the newest quick save. With no quick save yet, it says so.

Both are also buttons under Start, ACTIONS.

## The Start menu
Start opens a menu with eight big buttons: RESUME, MANUAL, SAVE GAME, LOAD GAME, SETTINGS, ACTIONS, LEFT HAND and EXIT GAME. Tap one, or use the D-pad and A. B or Start closes it. Each button has a one-line reminder of what it does on the top screen. Start works on the main menu too; there MANUAL, LEFT HAND and EXIT GAME can be used.
- SAVE GAME opens the save screen and LOAD GAME the load screen. SETTINGS opens Preferences. ACTIONS shows every button shortcut as a touch button. These four only work during play on the map; on the world map or over a window they are greyed out and say so.
- LEFT HAND on/off swaps the D-pad with Y, A, B and X (Y is up, A is right, B is down, X is left, and the reverse) so the stylus can be held in the left hand. It is remembered, and every button combination in this manual follows the swap. If the SD card refuses to store the setting, the menu says so.
[1]- EXIT GAME asks "Exit Fallout?" with YES and NO. The ring starts on NO and B is NO. After YES the game is saved into slot 10 and closes, back to the Homebrew Menu. Nothing is written before you say yes.
[2]- EXIT GAME asks "Exit Fallout 2?" with YES and NO. The ring starts on NO and B is NO. After YES the game is saved into slot 10 and closes, back to the Homebrew Menu. Nothing is written before you say yes.

Where EXIT GAME can save:
- On the map: it saves, then closes.
- With the inventory, Pip-Boy, Skilldex or automap open: it closes that window, saves, then closes the game.
- On the world map, in a conversation, when trading or on the character sheet it cannot save. It says "The game cannot be saved here" and the button reads EXIT ANYWAY; NO takes you back so you can save first.

If the save to slot 10 does not work (a full card, or the game refusing in the middle of something), the game says NOT SAVED and asks again before it closes. It never quits after a failed save without asking.

To save by hand, tap a slot and then Done. The 3DS keyboard opens with a description filled in: the name of the place for a new save, or the old description when you overwrite a slot. Tap OK to save, or type your own first. Cancel goes back to the slot list without saving.

A save that fails never costs you the old one: the game puts the previous save back and tells you the save failed instead of saying it worked.

Save often. Fallout is an unforgiving game.

## The exit save
Slot 10 is kept for the exit save. EXIT GAME writes it, and so does closing the game from the HOME menu while you are playing on the map (the game waits until the save is finished before it closes). Your own saves in slots 1 to 9 are never touched by it.

## Coming back
On the main menu tap LOAD GAME. The list opens on your newest save, which is the exit save in slot 10 if you left with EXIT GAME. Tap Done to load it.

## Where saves are kept
[1]sdmc:/3ds/fallout/DATA/SAVEGAME/SLOT01 to SLOT10.
[2]sdmc:/3ds/fallout2/data/SAVEGAME/SLOT01 to SLOT10.

# Preferences
Start, then SETTINGS, opens Preferences, where the game settings are changed. The labels under the knobs are small on the touch screen: press ZL for a sharp view on the top screen. This port ships with:
- Running always on: the character runs instead of walking.
- Combat speed fast, and your own moves sped up too.
[2]- Karma changes reported in the message log.
- Quick saves without a slot list or a name, kept to slots 7 to 9.

Text, violence level, subtitles, brightness and sound volume work as on the PC.

# Tips
- Hold R whenever you enter a new area. It shows every lootable body and item at once.
- Tap the enemy you want before attacking so the dashboard shows your chance to hit.
- Use Select+A in combat instead of opening the inventory: same cost, fewer taps. Select+B reloads the same way.
- Skill books, Stimpaks and ammo are worth more than they look. Check bodies.
- Rest with the Pip-Boy clock (Select+Y) to heal for free outside combat.
- Save before talking to anyone important and before every fight you can see coming.
- If you get lost on the map, press Select to center on your character.

# Installing
## Files on the SD card
[1]Put the program, fallout-ce.3dsx, directly in sdmc:/3ds/.
[1]Put the game files in sdmc:/3ds/fallout/:
[1]- MASTER.DAT and CRITTER.DAT from your Fallout install
[1]- the DATA folder from your Fallout install
[1]- fallout.cfg
[2]Put the program, fallout2-ce.3dsx, directly in sdmc:/3ds/.
[2]Put the game files in sdmc:/3ds/fallout2/:
[2]- master.dat, critter.dat and patch000.dat from your Fallout 2 install
[2]- the data and sound folders from your Fallout 2 install
[2]- fallout2.cfg and ddraw.ini

The data files come from the Windows GOG or Steam version. No game data is included with this port.

## Sound
Sound needs sdmc:/3ds/dspfirm.cdc, which you dump once with the DSP1 homebrew. Without it the game runs silently and movies play without sound.

## Updating over Wi-Fi
1. On the 3DS, open the Homebrew Launcher and start ftpd.
[1]2. From the PC, upload fallout-ce.3dsx to /3ds/ (port 5000).
[2]2. From the PC, upload fallout2-ce.3dsx to /3ds/ (port 5000).
3. Close ftpd and start the game from the Homebrew Launcher.

You never need to take the SD card out. Your saves are not touched by an update.

## Starting the game
Start it from the Homebrew Launcher. The intro movies play first: tap the screen to skip each one. The main menu shows the controls on the top screen.

# Troubleshooting
| No sound | Dump dspfirm.cdc to sdmc:/3ds/ with the DSP1 homebrew
| Movies silent | The same: movies need dspfirm.cdc for sound
| Lost the character | Press Select. If the map is zoomed in, Select+Down zooms out
| Picture looks odd | Select+L returns to the normal layout; ZL swaps the screens back
| Map loads slowly | Big maps take a few seconds to read from the SD card. A fast card helps
| Stuck in a menu | B backs out of menus; in a conversation, tap a reply. Start opens the Start menu anywhere
[2]| Reporting a bug | The game always logs its start, exit and failures to /3ds/fallout2/debug.log. An empty /3ds/fallout2/debug.txt switches on the detailed log (it rolls over at 1 MB)
